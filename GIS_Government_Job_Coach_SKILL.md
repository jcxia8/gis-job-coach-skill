# GIS Government Job Application & Interview Coach

A research-first, strategic approach to government GIS job applications and interview preparation. Built from real WA Government application campaigns and proven by a successful Level 7 government appointment.

## The 5 Non-Obvious Principles

These are what most applicants get wrong.

### 1. Open with THEIR reality — not YOUR motivation

The opening must name the organisation's budget, asset scale or operational consequence — not why you want the job.

**Wrong:** "I am passionate about GIS and drawn to this role because..."
**Right:** "DPIRD's plant biosecurity surveillance systems protect WA's $11 billion primary industries export economy. The decisions this role supports directly affect how the state detects, responds to and manages pest incursions."

The panel should think: *this person understands what's at stake.*

### 2. Never tell the panel their own job

Cut any sentence that explains what their organisation does or how your experience "translates directly" to their work.

State your evidence. Let the panel draw the connection themselves.

### 3. Use 5-Part STAR — not 4

Most people write: Situation → Action → Result

What panels actually score:
- **Situation + Goal** — why did it matter?
- **Action** — what YOU did (use "I" not "we")
- **Result** — quantified data and metrics
- **Impact** — what permanently changed for the team?
- **Echo Line** — repeat the criterion's exact words back

> "This demonstrates my proven ability to [insert exact criterion wording]..."

That one sentence does the panel's scoring for them.

### 4. Purge AI words before submitting

Remove: passionate about · spearheaded · unwavering commitment · leveraged · seamlessly · transformative

Replace every one with hard evidence and active verbs.

### 5. Check page count BEFORE grammar

Cut content first. Polish second. Always.

## Interview Preparation

### Before the interview

1. **Research the panel's published work** — papers, projects, strategic plans
2. **Identify their current challenge** — what problem are they trying to solve?
3. **Prepare one STAR story per criterion** — different examples for each
4. **Build a 30-60-90 day plan** — hand it to the panel at the end of your pitch
5. **Prepare reverse interview questions** — ask about team challenges, not salary

### The 4-Part "Tell Me About Yourself" Formula

**Part 1 — Who you are + most relevant result**
One sentence. Quantified. No fluff.

**Part 2 — What's at stake for THEM**
Name their operational reality before talking about yourself.

**Part 3 — Proof you've solved this exact problem before**
Map their challenge to your specific evidence.

**Part 4 — Show you've been thinking about their NEXT problem**
Research their work. Come prepared with a solution they didn't ask for.

> Don't walk into an interview to answer questions.
> Walk in to solve their problem.

### Mock Interview Practice with Claude

Ask Claude to:
- Run a mock interview as a government panel member
- Ask questions based on the job's selection criteria
- Give feedback on your STAR structure after each answer
- Correct unclear language or weak evidence
- Time your answers (aim for 2-3 minutes per criterion)
- Help improve pronunciation and clarity of key technical terms

Example prompt:
> "Act as a government panel interviewing me for a GIS Project Manager role. Ask me about [criterion]. After my answer, give me feedback on my STAR structure, evidence quality and any language to improve."

## Pre-Submission Checklist

- [ ] Opening anchors to employer's economic/operational reality — not your motivation
- [ ] No sentence tells the panel their own job
- [ ] No AI tells (passionate, spearheaded, unwavering, leveraged, seamlessly)
- [ ] Every criterion: Situation+Goal → Action → Result → Impact → Echo Line
- [ ] Closing uses "I bring" not "I would bring"
- [ ] Page count checked before grammar pass
- [ ] All numbers cross-checked between resume and cover letter
- [ ] All acronyms expanded on first use
- [ ] Only criteria listed in "How to Apply" have been addressed

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Leading with your motivation | Open with their budget/operational reality |
| Telling the panel their job | Cut it — state evidence only |
| Missing Situation and Goal | Always open each criterion with context |
| Missing Echo Line | End every criterion with criterion's exact words |
| AI words in closing | "consistent record of" not "unwavering commitment to" |
| Addressing all 7 criteria when only 3 are required | Read "How to Apply" carefully |
| Same project used across multiple criteria | Assign each project to one criterion only |
| Page count checked after grammar | Cut content first, polish second |
